About sheet recognition

Turn a photo of printed sheet music into a score you can edit, play, and save.

Getting a good result

  • Use a clear photo of a flat page, taken straight on.
  • Light the page evenly and avoid shadows or glare.
  • Keep all staff lines and barlines visible.

Recognition works best with clear printed music. Handwriting, curved pages, angled photos, and dense notation may not be read correctly.

Choosing a layout

All staves · auto layout reads the page and keeps connected pairs of staves together, such as the right and left hands of a piano score.

If a connecting brace is faint or missing, choose Grand staff · paired rows. For one instrument continuing down the page, choose Single-staff rows in sequence. You can also recognize an individual staff.

For scores with more than two simultaneous staves, use Open MusicXML. Lyrics and chord names are not imported from photos.

Reviewing your score

Recognition is experimental and may miss or misread symbols. Check the notes, accidentals, rests, rhythm, and time signature against the original photo. Ornaments and ties may also need correction.

Choose Edit sheet to make corrections, then Apply changes to update the score. Discard leaves the original score unchanged. Use Play sheet to hear the result on a grand piano.

Privacy and saving

Your photo is processed on your device and is never uploaded. The first conversion downloads about 100 MB of recognition data, plus the software needed to run it. Your browser can cache these downloads for later use when storage is available.

Photos and scores are kept in memory and are lost when you close or reload the page. Use Save MusicXML after applying your edits to keep a copy. You can reopen that file later.
